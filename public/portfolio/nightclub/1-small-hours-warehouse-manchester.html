The venue

A mill unit with the lights off

Unit 3 of Shuttle Works spun cotton until 1958. We kept the cast-iron columns and the glazed brick, hung a sound system from the beams and took most of the bulbs out.

The old weaving floor. A sprung timber deck laid over the original flagstones, eleven iron columns, and a booth that sits at floor level rather than up on a stage.

Door policy

House rules, in full

Read them before you travel. The door team apply the same eight rules to everybody, including our own guests.

Accepted ID (originals only)

  • Passport
  • Photocard driving licence
  • Card with the PASS hologram
  • Not accepted: photos, screenshots or photocopies of ID
Get tickets

  1. Over 18s only

    Every night, both rooms. Nobody under 18 is admitted, whoever they are with.

  2. Challenge 25

    If you look under 25 you will be asked for ID. We accept a passport, a photocard driving licence or a card carrying the PASS hologram. No photos or screenshots of ID.

  3. Searches and the door team

    Our door staff are SIA licensed. Everyone is searched as a condition of entry. The team may refuse admission or ask anyone to leave: we reserve the right of admission.

  4. Last entry 01:30, no re-entry

    Doors open at 22:00. Last entry is 01:30, ticket or not. Once you leave you cannot come back in; the smoking yard is inside the venue, so you never need to.

  5. No photos on the dancefloor

    A sticker covers your phone cameras at the door. Phones stay in pockets in both rooms. Take calls and messages in the yard or the corridor.

  6. Dress

    There is no dress code. Wear what you can dance in for six hours. No football colours, and nothing printed to offend.

  7. Zero tolerance of drugs

    Anyone found with illegal drugs is refused entry or removed and may be reported to the police. If you or a friend feel unwell, tell staff straight away. Your welfare comes first.

  8. Zero tolerance of harassment and discrimination

    No unwanted touching, staring or comments. No racism, sexism, homophobia, transphobia or ableism. Tell any member of staff: we believe you and we act on it.

Safety & welfare

Looked after, not watched

Two trained welfare staff work every night, separate from security. You will know them by the pale blue armbands.

Ask for Angela

Feeling unsafe, uncomfortable or stuck with someone? Ask any member of staff for Angela. They will take you somewhere quiet and help you get back to your friends or home, discreetly.

The Quiet Room

A staffed safe space beside the cloakroom: seats, low light, water, phone chargers and no questions. Come in for five minutes or for the rest of the night.

Think you’ve been spiked?

Tell staff or the welfare team straight away and stay with someone you trust. In an emergency call 999. To report it afterwards, call 101.

Free tap water

At every bar, with no purchase and no queue: jugs and cups sit at the end of each one. Staff will refill a bottle for you too.

Earplugs, strobe and haze

Free earplugs at the cloakroom and both bars. We use haze all night and strobe lighting in some sets. If that affects you, the welfare team will find you a better spot.

Getting you home

Flat battery, lost friends, no way back? The welfare desk has chargers and a phone, and someone will wait with you until your booked car arrives.

Getting home

We close at 04:00
  • Night buses

    Night services leave from Piccadilly Gardens, an 11-minute walk along Great Ancoats Street. Check times with Transport for Greater Manchester before you set off.

  • Taxis

    Use a licensed taxi or a private hire car you have booked. Never get into a car that pulls up and offers. The pick-up point is the lay-by opposite the side door, and staff will wait there with you.

  • Trams & trains

    The last trams have gone long before we close and the first trains leave Piccadilly after 05:00. Plan the journey home before the journey in.

  • Bikes & cars

    Twelve covered cycle stands in the yard, locked from doors to close. There is no venue parking; Blue Badge bays are on Bengal Street.

Please leave quietly.Our neighbours are asleep. Keep your voice down on Bengal Street and don’t hang about outside: it is how we keep our licence.

The small print

Privacy

Small Hours (Manchester) Ltd is the data controller. We use the details you give us to sell you tickets, to answer enquiries and, only if you tick the box, to send the monthly line-up email. We never sell or share them for marketing.

Enquiries are deleted after 12 months and ticket records after six years. To see, correct or delete your data, write to privacy@smallhours.example. You can also complain to the Information Commissioner’s Office.

Cookies

This site sets no advertising or analytics cookies. Fonts, photographs and the 3D library are loaded from third-party hosts, which will see your IP address when the page loads.
