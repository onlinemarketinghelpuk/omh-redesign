## Who we are

OMH sounds like a senior specialist who is firmly on the client's side. We
know Google Ads, SEO, AI and WordPress inside out. We tell people the truth
even when it costs us a sale, and then we get the job done.

In one line: the trusted expert in your corner who says it straight, explains
it plainly and follows through.

### What that looks like

- **Practitioners, not salespeople.** The people writing to clients do the
  work. We speak from experience with household-name brands, government bodies
  and regulated businesses, not from a script.
- **Commercially honest.** If something sits outside scope, won't work or
  isn't worth the money, we say so before we take payment.
- **Protective.** We spot risk early (security, tracking gaps, legal claims,
  wasted spend) and we stand between the client and the problem. We give them
  a clear choice, not a scare.
- **Calm under pressure.** Urgent issues get a steady, factual reply: what's
  happening, what we're doing, what they need to do.
- **Organised and accountable.** Every conversation ends with who does what,
  by when, and what it costs.
- **Courteous and warm.** Good British manners, a real person behind the
  inbox, and genuine thanks for people's time.

### What we are not

- **Not jargon-heavy.** Technical terms always come with a plain explanation
  of why they matter.
- **Not pushy.** Paid options sit alongside the free or do-it-yourself route.
- **Not corporate or hyped.** No buzzwords, no exclamation marks to sell.
- **Not vague.** We name the number, the price, the date and the owner.

### Where we sit

| Scale | OMH |
|---|---|
| Formal to casual | Professional and relaxed, flexing more formal for senior and regulated clients |
| Serious to playful | Mostly serious, with light warmth |
| Reserved to direct | Very direct |
| Technical to plain | Plain first, technical only when needed |
| Salesy to advisory | Strongly advisory |

## Our voice: six principles

### 1. Straight talk, said kindly

Say the uncomfortable thing early, then show the way forward.

> "I want to be straight with you: what you are after and what sits inside our
> current scope are two different things. Rather than half build something
> that won't do the job, here is a clear breakdown of your options."

### 2. Explain the why, in plain English

Translate the technical problem into what it means for the client's business.
Lead with "the short version" when the detail is heavy.

> "The short version: the agency is genuine, so this isn't a scam. But genuine
> doesn't mean the amount they've asked for is owed."

> "Google only reports on what it's told. That's the gap, and it's why this is
> a build rather than a setting someone switches on."

### 3. Protect the client

Stand between the client and the risk. Give a clear recommendation and do the
legwork for them.

> "Please don't feel pressured into paying straight away. We need them to
> substantiate the claim first. I've drafted a response you can send."

> "Any further correspondence, send straight on to me and I'll handle it."

### 4. Honest caveats, built in

Name the limits before the client discovers them. If we got something wrong,
own it plainly.

> "One honest caveat while we're here. Google can only tie a signup back to
> the original ad click for about three months."

> "We have double checked and the links now resolve correctly. Please pass on
> our apologies for the oversight."

### 5. Options, priced and clear

Offer choices with a price on each, and include the cheaper or do-it-yourself
route.

> "We can update this for you as a one-off for £250 + VAT, or set you up on a
> monthly maintenance plan. If you'd prefer to handle it yourself, your
> hosting provider should be able to help."

### 6. Own it and close the loop

Confirm what's done, show proof, and say what happens next.

> "We have completed the update and I have also checked that it is mobile
> responsive. Please see the screenshots below."

> "Let me know if I've captured everything correctly and I'll get moving."

## Matching the client

The voice never changes, but the formality does. Mirror the client's own
register, and when in doubt start one step more formal.

| | Everyday clients (hospitality, retail, trades, events) | Formal and regulated clients (government, maritime, finance, medical) |
|---|---|---|
| Greeting | "Hi [first name]," or "Hey," once you know them well | "Good morning/afternoon [name]," or "Hi [name], I hope you are well?" |
| Opening | Straight to the point | A short courtesy line, then the point |
| Language | Contractions, relaxed phrasing, the odd friendly touch | Full sentences, fewer contractions, no emoji, no slang |
| Confirmations | "All done, live on the 4th." | "Thank you for your email. This has been done for you." plus the live link or screenshots |
| Feedback rounds | "Let me know what you think." | "Of course this is just a first version and open to your feedback. Happy to arrange a call." |
| Mistakes | "Sorry, that one's on us, now fixed." | "We have double checked and corrected this. Please accept our apologies for the oversight." |

### Extra rules for formal and regulated clients

- **Respect the chain of command.** Share early drafts with your day-to-day
  contact first, then present to senior decision makers. Address senior people
  by name and title where they use one.
- **Precision matters.** Quote document titles, revision numbers, URLs and
  dates exactly as the client writes them. Confirm that the change is live
  "across all points" where it appears.
- **Speed and proof.** Urgent requests get a same-day reply confirming the
  change, with the live link or screenshots.
- **Keep sensitive matters off long threads.** For legal, copyright or
  compliance issues, start a fresh email to the right person so advice isn't
  buried in a chain that gets forwarded on.
- **Be decisive.** They rely on us for a clear recommendation, so give one,
  numbered, with the reasons. Then flag any wider risk you've spotted ("There
  is also a wider point worth raising...").
- **Mind the content.** Never publish or advise on regulated wording without
  the client's sign-off.

## Tone by situation

| Situation | Tone | Length | Must include |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quick confirmation (change done, report sent) | Brisk, polite | 1 to 3 lines | What was done, when it's live, proof (link or screenshot) |
| Call recap | Organised, collaborative | Medium | What we agreed, direction, "Your side" and "Our side" actions, any prices |
| Design or work for review | Open, confident | Short | What's attached or linked, what feedback we need, that it's a first version, offer of a call |
| Scope or expectation reset | Candid, calm, constructive | Long, with headings | What we do today, what we don't, priced options, one recommendation |
| Technical explanation | Teacherly, plain English | Medium | The problem, why it matters to them, the fix, what we need from them |
| Urgent risk (security, outage) | Calm, factual, reassuring | Medium | What's happening, what we're doing, what they need to do (often nothing), any cost |
| Legal or compliance threat | Protective, decisive | Medium to long | The short version, the facts in their favour, a numbered recommendation, a drafted reply |
| Money or invoices | Transparent, factual | Short to medium | The figures, what they covered, why, what happens next |
| Proposal or upsell | Advisory, low pressure | Medium | Options, prices + VAT, the free or DIY route, "just let me know" |
| Instructions for a non-technical client | Patient, step by step | Long | Numbered steps, what they don't need to worry about, what to send back |
| Chasing a reply | Light, helpful | 1 to 2 lines | "Just checking this arrived and whether you had any questions." |
| Personal or relationship | Warm, human | Short | Genuine thanks, a personal touch, no pitch |

## How we write emails

Every OMH email follows the same shape: greeting, the point, the detail, the
next step, sign-off.

- **Greeting:** match the client (see Matching the client).
- **Opener:** one line that gets to the point. "As promised...", "Thanks for
  the call earlier. Here's a recap of...", "I wanted to let you know
  about...". A courtesy line such as "I hope you are well" is fine, but keep
  it to one line.
- **Body:** for anything over five lines, use short headings (bold or
  CAPITALS), bullets for actions and numbered steps for instructions or
  recommendations.
- **Next step:** one clear line saying who does what. "Just reply to this
  email and let me know how you'd like to proceed."
- **Sign-off:** "Kind regards" followed by your OMH email signature.

### Subject lines

Plain and specific: "Round up of call", "Monthly SEO Report: October", "PHP
security update for your website". When replying to a client, keep their
subject line, including any reference or revision number.

## House style

- **UK English:** optimise, colour, organisation, programme, enquiry.
- **Prices** are always written `£450 + VAT`, with a comma for thousands
  (`£3,000 + VAT`).
- **Dates** are written "4th November" or "Tuesday 13th October". Never use US
  date order.
- **No em dashes.** Use commas, full stops, colons or brackets.
- **Contractions** are fine for everyday clients. Use fewer for formal
  clients.
- **Active voice:** "We have updated your site", not "Your site has been
  updated".
- Keep paragraphs to **three sentences at most**.
- **One job per thread.** Reply on each job's own thread so nothing gets lost.
- **Proof beats claims.** Link the live page, report, preview or screenshot.
- Use **"we"** for the team's work and **"I"** for your own commitments
  ("We've fixed it, and I'll check it again on Monday").
- **Proofread** every email before it goes. Typos undo the expert impression.

## Words we use and avoid

Use phrases that sound like a real expert talking. Drop anything that sounds
like an agency template or an AI tool.

| Say this | Not this |
|---|---|
| I want to be straight with you | We'd love to explore this exciting opportunity |
| The short version: | Please find below a comprehensive overview |
| Here is where we sit | At this point in time, we are positioned to |
| One honest caveat while we're here | Results may vary |
| That's the gap, and it's why... | This presents a challenge |
| On that basis, we recommend: | We'd suggest you might want to consider |
| Your side / Our side | Moving forward, stakeholders will align |
| As promised, please find attached | Just circling back / touching base |
| This has been done for you | Your request has been actioned |
| Happy to walk you through it on a call | Let's hop on a quick sync |
| Let me know if I've captured everything correctly | Hope this helps! |
| There is nothing you need to do right now | Don't worry, we've got you covered! |
| Please accept our apologies for the oversight | Unfortunately, mistakes happen |

**Never use:** leverage, synergy, unlock, elevate, seamless, robust, holistic,
empower, delve, game-changing, cutting-edge, materially, outsized, underpins,
"not just X but Y", or lists of three added for rhythm rather than meaning.

**Use with confidence:** enquiries, conversions, spend, cost per click, form
fills, leads, rankings, scope, sign off, one-off, monthly maintenance, test
page, live, go-live date.

```

Kind regards
```

### Call recap

```
Hi [Name],

What we're changing
- [Change]

Your side
- [Action]

Our side
- [Action]
- Pricing: [Item] £[X] + VAT

Let me know if I've captured everything correctly and I'll get moving.

Kind regards
```

### Work for review

```
Hi [Name],

I hope you are well. Please find attached [what], ready for your feedback.

[One or two lines on the thinking behind it.] Of course this is a first
version and open to your feedback.

Let me know your thoughts, and I'm happy to arrange a call to talk it through.

Kind regards
```

### Scope reset

```
Hi [Name],

I've looked at this from a few angles and I want to be straight with you:
[what they want] and what sits in our current scope are two different things.
Rather than half build something that won't do the job, here is where we sit.

WHAT WE DO TODAY
[Current scope]

YOUR OPTIONS
1. [Option A]: [what it gives], £[X] + VAT
2. [Option B]: [what it gives], £[X] + VAT
3. [Free or DIY route]

WHAT I'D RECOMMEND
[One recommendation and why]

Happy to talk it through on our call [day].

Kind regards
```

### Risk or legal alert

```
Hi [Name],

I wanted to let you know about [issue].

The short version: [one or two plain sentences on what it is and what it
means for them].

What we've found
- [Fact in their favour]
- [Fact]

On that basis, we recommend:
1. [Action, and whether we've already done it]
2. [Action, with a drafted reply attached if needed]

There is nothing else you need to do right now. Any further correspondence,
send straight on to me.

Kind regards
```

## Before and after

**Before:** "Hi! We've noticed some challenges with your tracking. Our team
would love to explore a holistic solution to unlock the full potential of your
data. Let us know!"

**After:** "Hi [Name], Your enquiry forms send people off your website to fill
them in, so Google Ads isn't recording any of them as conversions. That's the
gap. The fix is a one-off build at £450 + VAT, and I'm happy to walk you
through it on tomorrow's call."

**Before:** "I don't deal with that side of the business."

**After:** "Invoices sit with our accounts team, who are copied in and will
send the full breakdown. In the meantime, here is a summary of the ad spend so
far this year."

## Before you send

Run through these before anything goes out under the OMH name.

- Does the first line say why I'm writing?
- Have I matched the client's level of formality?
- Have I been honest about limits, risks or scope?
- Would a non-technical reader understand every sentence?
- Is every price written as £X + VAT, with a cheaper or DIY option where one
  exists?
- Is it clear who does what next, and by when?
- Have I linked proof for any work I say is done?
- Are names, titles, document references and dates exactly right?
- UK spelling, no em dashes, nothing from the never-use list?
- Is it as short as it can be while still answering everything?
- Have I proofread it once, start to finish?
